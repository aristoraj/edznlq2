02

Where We Are Finding Real Value in AI

This quarter, our AI work focused on two practical opportunities: helping organisations strengthen their data-protection readiness and exploring how Voice AI can support last-mile program needs.

DPDP Comply · ZolaShield

Making Data Protection Readiness Easier

From a 15-minute conversation to a practical compliance roadmap

After completing a successful pilot with five organisations, we launched the ZolaShield DPDP Comply Cohort with Waste Warriors Society and Unnati Foundation.

The four-month program helps each organisation understand how personal data is collected, processed, stored, shared, retained and deleted, then turn those findings into a practical compliance roadmap.

The journey begins with a 15-minute Voice AI-powered assessment. Instead of lengthy questionnaires or compliance checklists, ZolaShield conducts an adaptive conversation and maps how data moves across the organisation.

This helps teams identify priority risks, clarify internal ownership and understand what actions should come next.

Voice AI · AI Clinic

Listening at Scale with Zolabs

Moving from an initial idea to a practical use case

We completed the first AI Clinic for Social Impact Organisations, testing Voice AI use cases with more than six organisations. The conversations explored how AI calling could support beneficiary feedback, follow-up surveys and program data collection.

Each participating organisation received a walkthrough of Zolabs.ai focused on its specific requirements. The clinic helped teams identify where Voice AI could create practical value in their work.

We plan to bring the AI Clinic to more social-impact organisations. If you have a use case to explore, we would love to hear from you.

Kalvi Kural 2026

Can AI Truly Understand the Reality of Indian Classrooms?

At Kalvi Kural 2026, organised by the Madhi Foundation, Akshay joined voices from civil society, government, Tech4Good and government-school teachers to explore the role of AI in Indian classrooms.

The conversation highlighted the need to build technology around what teachers and students genuinely need, not what we assume they need.

As AI advances, children must be prepared not only to use it, but also to understand, question and eventually build it. This means strengthening foundational learning, improving the rigour of STEM education and preserving the human joy of teaching and learning.

Charcha 2026

Learning Across Samaaj, Sarkaar and Bazaar

At Charcha 2026, Akshay and Shubham joined leaders and practitioners from civil society, government and business to discuss some of India’s most complex development challenges.

The gathering offered an opportunity to understand how different parts of the ecosystem approach technology, collaboration and scale, and how stronger partnerships can help proven solutions reach more communities.

A personal highlight was reconnecting with Alina Alam and the Mitti Café team. When Akshay first showed Alina a solution EdZola had built for the café in 2020, she exclaimed, “Akshay, ye kya jadoo kar diya tumne!”

Six years later, Akshay and Shubham met the team again, including persons with disabilities building livelihoods through the café. It was a reminder of the people behind the work and the relationships that continue beyond a project.

Listening Across Cities

Learning Directly from Organisations and Sector Leaders

Across Chennai, Delhi, Bengaluru and Pune, we connected with People4Good, ILSS, Tapasya, Mudita, TRRAIN, and members of the Chevening and wider development-sector communities. We also participated in the ILSS Chennai Foundation Day.

These conversations reinforced the need for technology that is practical and easy to adopt, continued listening as organisations evolve, and partnerships that create space to think together about sustainability, data, livelihoods, jobs and the future of AI.

Thank you to everyone who welcomed us and shared their experiences openly. We look forward to continuing these conversations and connecting with more organisations in the months ahead.

Together, these experiences showed us that communities are built in many ways, through public dialogue, informal gatherings, sector platforms and focused conversations. Each space helped us listen more closely, learn from others and strengthen the relationships that make lasting change possible.
